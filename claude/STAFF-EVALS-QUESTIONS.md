# Staff Self-Evaluation — the questions

Exactly what staff see, by role. Source of truth: `FORMS` in `supabase/functions/staff-evals/index.ts`.

**First screen:** What guests are saying: shared Google reviews from the last 60 days (reviews naming them, the 5 most recent complaints, two 5-star), with management's "what we're changing" under each.

**Intro on every form:** This is your self-evaluation. It is not a test and it is not a write-up. For each standard, rate yourself honestly and give us one real example from a shift. Management rates the same standards, then you'll see both side by side and we'll talk about where to go from here. Honest 3s are more useful to us than 5s across the board.

**Rating scale (every standard):** 1 Not yet · 2 Some shifts · 3 Most shifts · 4 Every shift · 5 I could train it

**Every standard also asks for a real example from a shift (8+ words).**

## Server Self-Evaluation

**1. Greet and pace**
Standard: Every new table greeted within 1 minute. Drinks down within 5 minutes. Check back within 2 minutes of food landing.
Example prompt: Tell us about a busy stretch: did you hit those times? If not, where did it slip?

**2. Handheld ordering**
Standard: Every order goes on the handheld at the table. The terminal is only for closing out a check, or when no handheld is available.
Example prompt: When was the last time you rang at the terminal instead of the handheld, and why?

**3. Full hands, in and out**
Standard: Never walk into or out of the building empty-handed. The building is in and out. If your items aren't up, run someone else's.
Example prompt: Describe what you carried in and out on your last few trips through the building.

**4. "Not my table" doesn't exist**
Standard: Drinks on the counter get run by whoever sees them. Dirty tables get bussed by whoever walks by. A guest at another server's table who flags you is your guest.
Example prompt: Give an example of helping a table or a teammate that wasn't yours.

**5. Over-communicate with guests**
Standard: Tell the table about a delay before they have to ask. An upset guest is the #1 priority. Go to them right away, apologize, own it, fix it, and bring in a manager. Never hide from a table.
Example prompt: Tell us about the last time a table was unhappy or waiting too long. What did you do?

**6. Allergies and accuracy**
Standard: Never guess on an allergy. Check with the kitchen and a manager, flag the ticket, and come back to the guest with a clear answer.
Example prompt: How do you handle an allergy question when you're not 100% sure of the answer?

**7. Sell The Quarry**
Standard: Mention tonight's special, upcoming events and golf at every greet. Invite guests back.
Example prompt: What do you actually say at the greet? Write it the way you'd say it.

**8. Leaving the floor and cashing out**
Standard: Tell a manager or owner before you leave the floor for any reason. Side work and your checklist signed off. Cash settled to the closing manager on the cash-out sheet with both sets of initials.
Example prompt: Walk us through how you close out your night.

## Host Self-Evaluation

**1. Own the door**
Standard: Every guest is greeted the moment they walk in. The host stand is never left empty during service without someone covering it.
Example prompt: Tell us about a time the door got busy. How fast did guests get greeted?

**2. Seating rotation**
Standard: Seat sections evenly in rotation. Tell the server every time you seat them. Don't double-seat a section unless that server says they're ready.
Example prompt: How do you decide where the next party goes when it's busy?

**3. Wait times and the waitlist**
Standard: Quote honest wait times. Check in with waiting guests before they come to you. Know which tables are about to open.
Example prompt: Describe the last time you had a wait. What did you quote and how close was it?

**4. Phone and reservations**
Standard: Never book a phone reservation yourself. Point callers to the website, or take their details and pass them to a manager or owner to confirm.
Example prompt: What do you say when someone calls to make a reservation?

**5. Know tonight**
Standard: Know tonight's special, upcoming events, golf, and any private-event or wedding closures, so you can answer any guest question at the door.
Example prompt: What's coming up at The Quarry in the next two weeks? How do you find out?

**6. Support the floor**
Standard: When the door is quiet, help reset tables, refill waters, run menus and help the bussers. Standing at the stand on a slow stretch isn't the job.
Example prompt: What do you do when nobody is at the door?

**7. Checklist and side work**
Standard: Host & Bus board signed off for your shift. Menus clean, stand stocked, entry tidy.
Example prompt: Which part of the checklist do you skip or rush most often?

## Busser Self-Evaluation

**1. Hold your station**
Standard: Station 1 is inside, Station 2 is outside. Stay posted in your area until your break. Tell a manager before you leave it.
Example prompt: On your last shift, how much of the night were you actually in your station?

**2. Scout your tables**
Standard: Keep eyes on every table in your area. Clear empties as soon as guests are done with them. No table sits dirty.
Example prompt: How do you know a table needs you before the server asks?

**3. Fast resets**
Standard: When a table leaves: clear, wipe, reset and tell the host it's ready, fast.
Example prompt: Walk us through a reset, start to finish.

**4. Back up the servers**
Standard: You're the servers' support. Refill waters, pre-bus, run food when it's up, and ask servers what they need.
Example prompt: Give an example of something you did that made a server's night easier.

**5. Full hands, in and out**
Standard: Never walk into or out of the building empty-handed.
Example prompt: What do you carry out to the floor when you head back out?

**6. Guest manners**
Standard: Clear politely ("May I take these?"), never reach over guests, and pass any guest request to their server right away.
Example prompt: A guest asks you for something while you're clearing. What do you do?

**7. Checklist and closing duties**
Standard: Host & Bus board signed off. Closing duties done before you clock out.
Example prompt: Which closing duty gets skipped most often, and why?

## Expo Self-Evaluation

**1. Check every plate**
Standard: Every plate is checked against the ticket before it leaves the window: mods, sides, sauces, and allergies.
Example prompt: When did you last catch a mistake before it went out? What was it?

**2. Allergy tickets**
Standard: Allergy tickets are confirmed with the line and handed off clearly. If anything is unclear, stop and get a manager. Never guess.
Example prompt: Walk us through what happens when an allergy ticket comes up.

**3. Timing**
Standard: A table's food goes out together. Food doesn't die in the window. Call for runners the moment a table is up.
Example prompt: Tell us about a night the window backed up. What did you do?

**4. Talk to the line**
Standard: Clear, calm calls to the kitchen. When tickets run long, tell the servers so they can warn their tables before the guest asks.
Example prompt: How do you let servers know their table's food is running behind?

**5. Presentation**
Standard: Rims wiped, garnish right, hot food hot. If it doesn't look right, it goes back.
Example prompt: What do you check on a plate in the few seconds before it goes out?

**6. Run it right**
Standard: Table and seat numbers on every run. No auctioning food ("Who had the burger?"). Pick up empties on the way back.
Example prompt: How do you know which guest gets which plate?

**7. Window and checklist**
Standard: Window and expo station kept clean and stocked through service. Checklist signed off.
Example prompt: What's the state of the window at the end of your shift?

## Bartender Self-Evaluation

**1. Bar and service-bar speed**
Standard: Bar guests are greeted fast. Server tickets are made promptly. Nothing dies on the counter. If you're buried, call for help.
Example prompt: Tell us about the last time you got buried. What happened to the server tickets?

**2. Responsible service**
Standard: When in doubt, card them. Stop serving a guest who's visibly intoxicated and tell a manager. No overpours.
Example prompt: Describe a time you cut someone off or carded someone. How did you handle it?

**3. Pours and recipes**
Standard: Jigger every pour. Make every cocktail to the recipe as written. Log waste and spills.
Example prompt: Which drinks do you free-pour or eyeball, if any?

**4. Ring everything**
Standard: Every drink is rung in Square before it's poured. No free drinks. Comps need a manager's approval.
Example prompt: When was the last time a drink went out that wasn't rung? What happened?

**5. Wine and beer service**
Standard: Bottles are presented, opened and poured properly. Beer buckets and bottles go out cold and correct.
Example prompt: How do you present and open a bottle of wine for a table?

**6. Bar guests and selling**
Standard: Talk up specials, upcoming events, the wine club and golf. Bar regulars know your name and you know theirs.
Example prompt: What do you tell a new bar guest about The Quarry?

**7. Cash and tabs**
Standard: Drawer counted in and out. Tabs closed before you leave. Manager tabs rung correctly. Cash-out follows the procedure.
Example prompt: Walk us through how you close your drawer and tabs.

**8. Clean bar and checklist**
Standard: Bar stays clean and stocked through service. Opening, hourly and closing tasks on the Bar board signed off.
Example prompt: Which checklist task gets skipped most when it's busy?

## Kitchen Self-Evaluation

**1. Food safety**
Standard: Temps checked and logged. Everything labeled and dated. First in, first out. Hands washed and gloves changed between tasks.
Example prompt: What's one food-safety step you've seen skipped on a busy night?

**2. Allergies**
Standard: Allergy tickets get a clean surface, clean tools and a fresh pan. If you're not sure, ask the chef. Never guess.
Example prompt: Walk us through how you cook an allergy ticket.

**3. Recipes and plating**
Standard: Every plate matches the spec: portion, temp, plating. Taste and check before it goes to the window.
Example prompt: Which dish is hardest to keep consistent, and why?

**4. Ticket times and calls**
Standard: Call back to expo. Say "behind" and "hot". Tell expo the moment your station is running long so the servers can warn their tables.
Example prompt: What do you do when your station falls behind?

**5. Prep and 86s**
Standard: Prep list done to par before service. If something is about to run out, tell the chef and expo right away so it gets 86'd before a server sells it.
Example prompt: When did something last run out mid-service? How was it handled?

**6. Clean as you go**
Standard: Station clean through service. Dish keeps up so the line never runs out of plates or pans. Closing checklist signed off.
Example prompt: What does your station look like at the end of a Saturday?

**7. One kitchen**
Standard: Help the next station when you're caught up, line, prep or dish. No "that's not my job".
Example prompt: Give an example of jumping in to help another station.

**8. Product and waste**
Standard: Store product properly, rotate it, and log waste. Don't throw out what could be used, and don't serve what shouldn't be.
Example prompt: How do you handle product that's close to its date?

## Open questions (every role, 15+ words each)

1. Look at what guests have been saying (the reviews at the start, or what you've heard on the floor). Pick one complaint. What will you personally do differently so it doesn't happen at your tables or your station?
2. Where are you strongest right now? Give us a real example from a recent shift.
3. Which of these standards is hardest to hit on a busy night, and why? What would make it easier?
4. What do you need from management (training, tools, scheduling, communication) to do your job better?

## After management responds

The employee sees both ratings side by side with notes, strengths and a 30-day focus, then writes: *What will you work on? Tell us in your own words what you'll change over the next 30 days and anything you disagree with.* (15+ words) and acknowledges.
